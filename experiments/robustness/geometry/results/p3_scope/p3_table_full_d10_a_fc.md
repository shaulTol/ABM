# P3 scope controls (full_d10_a_fc)

**Table (P3 scope controls).** Finite-horizon SSA Ŝ and DA diagnostics. Ŝ is the *finite-horizon* estimator (NOT the resolved population SSA): lag 0 excluded, noise floor = 1/N = 0.0005 (N=2000 trajectories), stopping lag τ* = largest lag with ρ²>1/N, Ŝ = Δt·Σ_{τ=1}^{τ*} ρ²(τ), Δt=0.01, horizon H=20. SSA over 3 seeds; DA over seeds [0, 1, 2, 3, 4]. λ1/λ2≈1 indicates a (near-)degenerate top-2 DA subspace; in that regime individual DA directions are non-unique, which is what the large cross-seed DA1 rotation measures. dip/Silverman p are for the DA1 projection (small p ⇒ reject unimodality). **τ\*=max_lag ⇒ window-limited**: ρ² never fell below the floor inside the horizon, so Ŝ is truncated by the WINDOW, not the noise floor, and is a finite-horizon LOWER BOUND (shown as ≥), not a resolved value. The top-2 DA subspace column is a genuine 2-of-d measurement only for rows with d>2; rows at d=2 are marked n/a because there the top-2 subspace is all of R² by construction (0° is definitional, not evidence). A DA λ1/λ2 entry of 'n/a (λ2≤0)' means the second eigenvalue of the symmetrized lagged autocovariance is non-positive, i.e. there is no second slow mode and a ratio is ill-defined — expected for a unimodal density. The matched baseline is an isotropic Gaussian of the same dimension and covariance scale (σ²=tr(Σ_f)/d, which also matches the diffusion coefficient), run with exactly the same N, Δt, horizon and stopping rule. In 10D the four circular modes occupy span(e1,e2) and the remaining eight coordinates are FAST Gaussians (variance 0.05 vs 0.5 in-plane, ≈10× the in-plane relaxation rate), so the known slow plane is span(e1,e2) and recovering it is nontrivial. λ2/λ3≫1 is what makes the slow SUBSPACE identifiable (Davis–Kahan) even when DA1 rotates freely inside the near-degenerate block.
